A narrow editorial purpose

Bracket & Bay is a fictional independent publication built as a static editorial demonstration. It focuses on the interfaces among desks, mounts, screens, laptops, cables, and users. It is not a retailer, manufacturer, engineering firm, testing laboratory, standards body, installer, or safety certifier. The publication does not operate a product inventory or claim access to unreleased equipment.

A fictional editor, stated plainly

Rowan Vale is a fictional editor identity used to keep the issue’s voice consistent. The name, portrait, biography, and example email do not identify a real employee or credential holder. No award, professional registration, laboratory, audience size, or client history is implied. The visual portrait is generated editorial artwork, not documentary evidence of a real person.

Our method

The method begins with a physical interface: the desktop beneath a clamp, the active area of a screen, the vents beneath a laptop, or the connector at the end of a cable. We separate manufacturer specifications from desk measurements and label uncertainty rather than filling it with a performance promise. The articles favor reversible observations, single-variable adjustments, written records, and a clear stop condition.

What we do not claim

No named product was purchased, installed, load-tested, endurance-tested, or measured for this issue. Category examples explain tradeoffs; they are not disguised rankings. Readers should confirm current dimensions, capacity limits, attachment instructions, warranty terms, furniture constraints, and local requirements through authoritative sources before acting.

How to use the publication

Use these pages as a planning notebook and question set. A good outcome is not a dramatic photograph or the maximum possible reach. It is a support path that can be explained, a screen geometry that fits the task, a laptop that stays ventilated and retained, cables that move without strain, and an arrangement that can be inspected. Qualified assistance is appropriate whenever equipment, furniture, or consequences exceed a reader’s knowledge.

Editorial boundaries

When an article names a sequence, it describes a way to organize observations rather than a guarantee of fit or safety. Real equipment changes over time, and apparently similar furniture can differ internally. Bracket & Bay therefore keeps claims close to visible interfaces and directs readers back to current manufacturer material, workplace procedures, and qualified support for consequential decisions. The publication earns trust by narrowing its claim, not by inventing authority.
