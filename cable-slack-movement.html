Route Cables for the Arm’s Full Motion Envelope

A movement-first method for planning power and signal slack, strain relief, bend radius, and clean travel through every arm position.

Guide by Rowan Vale · Cables are moving members in an adjustable workstation. This guide follows each lead from fixed endpoint to moving connector so neat routing does not quietly create strain, drag, pinch points, or a short travel limit.

Inventory every moving connection

List monitor power, video, laptop power, dock, network, audio, camera, and peripheral leads. Mark which endpoints move with the arm and which remain fixed. This separates cables that need a travel loop from those that should stay anchored to the desk.

For the specific checkpoint “inventory every moving connection,” Follow this lead with one hand near the moving connector while the other moves a single pivot slowly. Observe when a service loop opens, closes, twists, or brushes a hinge, and repeat at maximum desk height where applicable. Cable behavior must be witnessed through travel; a tidy still image says nothing about hidden pull at the far reach.

Find the farthest connector distance

Respect bend radius and connector direction

Cable management changes friction and the center of pull. Run the complete motion check after covers, clips, and ties are closed. If the arm begins to drift or return to one position, release the hidden cable force rather than compensating only with more joint tension.

Close the guide with an acceptance record

A successful route stays relaxed through the complete intended motion while keeping connectors free from load. Keep the record close to this guide’s subject and compare it with the current equipment whenever the workstation changes.

Treat visual tidiness as the result of correct travel planning, not the first objective. If a bundle fights the mechanism, the path needs revision. Date the conclusion and identify any unresolved point plainly; an acceptance record is a working boundary, never a product certification.
